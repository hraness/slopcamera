# Direct a finished piece

Use this for a creative brief, a showcase, or an open-ended request for polished media. Pair it with the workflow reference for the selected engine. Confirm commands through the installed CLI; this guide does not add operations.

## Choose a coherent result

State the medium, intended display, subject, visual treatment, and delivery size. For motion, specify duration, opening, change, and ending. For a still, specify the focal point and what should be understood at a glance. Resolve routine choices from the user's context and proceed; ask only when a material missing decision prevents useful work.

Translate the treatment into concrete rules: composition, silhouettes, material behavior, a restrained palette, lighting direction, type hierarchy, camera, and movement. Use [visual style direction](visual-style-direction.md) for animation families and output resolution. Use supplied references to identify particular qualities, preserving rights and identity constraints.

## Prove the difficult part first

Render a representative still or short motion test before committing to a long export. Pick the moment most likely to fail: glass with visible contents, interacting objects, a character's key pose, a complex fold, readable mathematics, or a difficult footage crop. Inspect actual pixels at the intended viewing size. A successful process exit is not creative admission.

If the concept reads only after an explanation, simplify its staging. If a material is wrong, adjust geometry, shader, and lighting together. More samples or higher resolution will not repair composition. Preserve failed attempts when they explain a consequential choice.

## Shape the sequence

Give motion a purpose and a clear progression. Stage anticipation before an important action, control acceleration, and leave a readable final hold. Keep cuts, camera movement, type, and sound in agreement. Inspect beginning, middle, transitions, and ending; test continuity where loops or cuts require it.

Use sound deliberately when the requested medium benefits from it. Keep music, ambience, effects, and narration as separate editable inputs. Place accents against actual picture events, preserve intelligibility, avoid clipping, and finish the tail. Check true peaks, loudness, fade envelopes, and timing. Listen when the environment supports audio input; otherwise explicitly record that review was limited to signal and structural checks. Never claim to have listened from file metadata.

## Make a visible revision

Retain the initial source and request. Change one meaningful decision, render again, and compare the same moment in both versions. Useful directions include:

- “Give the moon more room”: increase its scale, open the composition, and hold departure until the sign is readable.
- “Make the bottle feel colder”: change material roughness, reflection shape, condensation, and light color together.
- “Let the joke land”: move the accent to the action and preserve a beat after the payoff.
- “Explain the added harmonic”: isolate its contribution before combining it with the existing waveform.

Do not silently substitute an unrelated generated take for an edit the source can express. Describe what is independently editable and which changes need regenerated input. Keep original assets, parameter variants, project decisions, and output lineage.

## Review the delivered version

Inspect the actual delivery encode and crop, not only the master. Look for clipped subjects or type, broken silhouettes, aliasing, banding, unintended collisions, motion jumps, and a weak opening or ending. Check captions and labels at phone size. Verify format, duration, resolution, audio presence, and source paths against the exported file.

For public examples, link finished work to the brief, source, exact invocation, prerequisites, and a useful revision. State when source requires a repository checkout or when a provider is billed separately. Originality, clarity, and intentional finishing decide whether the piece is worth featuring.
